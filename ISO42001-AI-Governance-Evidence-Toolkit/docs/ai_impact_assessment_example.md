# AI Impact Assessment Example

## Scenario

A small e-commerce business uses AI assistance to draft Shopify product descriptions, SEO snippets, category text and first-draft customer-support responses.

## Business purpose

The organisation wants to reduce time spent on repetitive content drafting while improving consistency across product pages and customer communication.

## AI use cases in scope

| Use case | Description | Risk level |
|---|---|---|
| AI-assisted Shopify product content | Draft product descriptions and SEO copy | Medium |
| Customer support response drafting | Draft replies for staff review | Medium |
| Internal policy summarisation | Summarise internal documents for onboarding | Low |

## Stakeholders

- customers,
- store visitors,
- support staff,
- marketing/e-commerce staff,
- business owner or management team.

## Data considerations

The organisation should avoid entering unnecessary personal data, confidential supplier information, payment data, authentication data, or customer-specific sensitive details into AI tools.

Recommended approach:

1. use public product information where possible,
2. remove unnecessary identifiers from support examples,
3. keep original source material available for human verification,
4. document which tools are approved for use.

## Key risks

| Risk | Example | Recommended control |
|---|---|---|
| Inaccurate claims | AI invents product benefits or compatibility | Human review and claim-verification checklist |
| Confidentiality issue | Staff paste unnecessary customer details | Data-minimisation guidance |
| Over-reliance | Staff send AI draft without checking | Mandatory review before external use |
| Poor transparency | Organisation cannot explain where AI is used | Maintain AI use-case register |
| Supplier uncertainty | AI tool terms are not reviewed | Approved-tool and supplier-review process |

## Human oversight

Human review is required before:

- publishing product content,
- sending customer-facing responses,
- using AI-generated summaries for policy or compliance purposes,
- making decisions that affect customers, staff or suppliers.

## Evidence examples

- AI use-case register entry,
- AI policy acknowledgment,
- product-claim review checklist,
- customer-support review procedure,
- approved-tool list,
- periodic review record.

## Residual risk

Residual risk is acceptable for low-impact drafting use cases if:

- AI outputs are reviewed,
- sensitive data is minimised,
- claims are verified,
- the organisation keeps a record of material AI use cases and controls.

## Recommended next steps

1. Create an approved-tool list.
2. Add a checklist for product claims and customer-support drafts.
3. Review AI use cases quarterly.
4. Add supplier due-diligence notes for AI tools used in business workflows.
5. Train staff on safe AI use and escalation expectations.
