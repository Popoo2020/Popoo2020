# Draft Internal AI Use Policy

> Template for SME AI governance discussions. This is not legal advice and should be adapted to the organisation's sector, data categories, contractual duties and regulatory obligations.

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines basic expectations for responsible, secure and reviewable use of AI tools inside the organisation.

The goal is to support useful AI adoption while reducing avoidable risks related to confidentiality, accuracy, fairness, accountability and uncontrolled tool use.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, contractors and teams using AI tools for work-related tasks, including text generation, summarisation, customer-support drafting, e-commerce content, internal analysis, workflow assistance and security or operations support.

## 3. Approved use

AI tools may be used for:

- drafting internal documents,
- summarising non-sensitive information,
- generating marketing drafts for human review,
- preparing first-draft customer-support responses,
- brainstorming process improvements,
- assisting with structured analysis where a human remains accountable.

## 4. Restricted use

Users must not use AI tools to:

- enter unnecessary personal, confidential or sensitive information,
- make final decisions about people without human review,
- publish customer-facing claims without verification,
- bypass security controls or approval workflows,
- rely on AI output as legal, financial, medical or professional advice without qualified review,
- connect unauthorised AI tools to internal systems or datasets.

## 5. Human review

AI-generated outputs must be reviewed before they are used for:

- customer communication,
- employment or access-related decisions,
- compliance documentation,
- security workflow changes,
- public marketing claims,
- contractual or legal-facing materials.

The human reviewer remains responsible for the final output.

## 6. Data minimisation

Users should provide only the minimum information required for the AI task. Where possible, direct identifiers, unnecessary details and confidential content should be removed or generalised before use.

## 7. Transparency and records

Teams should keep records of material AI use cases, including:

- business purpose,
- owner,
- tool or supplier,
- type of data involved,
- affected users or stakeholders,
- review requirements,
- key risks and controls.

## 8. Escalation

If an AI output appears harmful, biased, inaccurate, unsafe, or outside the user's authority, the user should stop using the output and escalate to the relevant process owner or manager.

## 9. Review cycle

This policy should be reviewed at least annually, or sooner if the organisation adopts new AI tools, changes business-critical workflows, or identifies a material AI-related incident.
