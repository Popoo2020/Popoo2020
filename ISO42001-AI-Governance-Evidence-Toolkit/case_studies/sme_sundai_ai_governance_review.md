# Client-Style Case Study: Sundai AI Governance Readiness Review

> **Case-study type:** anonymised / simulated client-style engagement for portfolio demonstration.  
> This is not presented as a real paid client engagement, legal advice, or a formal ISO/IEC 42001 audit. It demonstrates how the toolkit can be applied to a realistic SME using an AI product or internal AI assistant such as **Sundai AI**.

## Client scenario

A small B2B services company uses **Sundai AI** as an AI-assisted workflow tool for internal knowledge work, customer-response drafting, marketing support and operational summarisation.

The company benefits from faster drafting and analysis, but does not yet have a formal AI governance process. Management wants to understand:

- which AI use cases exist,
- what data is being entered,
- which outputs require human review,
- whether supplier/tool review is needed,
- what evidence should be retained,
- and which governance gaps should be prioritised before broader rollout.

## Engagement objective

Perform a lightweight **AI Governance Readiness Review** using the ISO/IEC 42001-oriented toolkit.

The objective is not to certify the company. The objective is to create a practical evidence baseline and management view of AI-related risks, controls and next steps.

## AI use cases reviewed

| Use case | Business purpose | Initial risk level | Human oversight expectation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer response drafting | Draft first-pass replies for staff review | Medium | Staff approval before sending |
| Marketing content drafting | Draft website, SEO and campaign text | Medium | Marketing review before publishing |
| Internal knowledge summarisation | Summarise internal notes and policies | Low to Medium | Source verification before reuse |
| Operational workflow summarisation | Summarise tasks, updates and process notes | Medium | Process owner review |
| Recruitment or people-related notes | Structure role-fit notes for human review | High indicator | Formal review before use or expansion |

## Key governance questions

### 1. AI use-case visibility

**Observation:** The company uses Sundai AI in several workflows, but the uses are not consistently recorded.

**Risk:** Shadow AI usage may grow without clear ownership, data-handling expectations or review requirements.

**Recommended control:** Maintain an AI use-case register with owner, purpose, tool, data category, affected stakeholders, risk level and evidence reference.

### 2. Data minimisation

**Observation:** Staff may enter more context than needed when asking the AI tool to draft or summarise.

**Risk:** Confidential, personal or unnecessary operational details may be shared with the AI tool without a clear business need.

**Recommended control:** Add a data-minimisation rule: remove unnecessary identifiers and confidential details before AI use unless explicitly approved.

### 3. Supplier and tool review

**Observation:** Sundai AI is used as a business tool, but supplier review evidence is not yet documented.

**Risk:** The company may not know enough about data retention, training on user inputs, processing location, access controls, audit logs, subprocessors or incident notification.

**Recommended control:** Complete the AI supplier due-diligence checklist before expanding use.

### 4. Human oversight

**Observation:** Human review happens informally, but the review requirement is not documented for each use case.

**Risk:** AI-generated output may be used too directly, especially for customer-facing or people-impacting workflows.

**Recommended control:** Define human oversight expectations in the use-case register and maintain a human oversight decision log for higher-risk workflows.

### 5. People-impacting workflows

**Observation:** The company is considering using AI assistance to structure recruitment or role-fit notes.

**Risk:** Employment-related AI support can raise fairness, transparency and legal/compliance concerns.

**Recommended control:** Treat people-impacting use as a high-risk indicator. Do not deploy or expand without formal legal/compliance review, documented criteria and human decision-maker accountability.

## Risk snapshot

| Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Residual risk after basic controls | Recommended next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inaccurate customer-facing content | Medium | Medium | Low to Medium | Add review and claim-verification checklist |
| Unapproved or undocumented AI use | High | Medium | Medium | Maintain AI use-case register |
| Excessive data sharing | High | Medium | Medium | Add data-minimisation guidance |
| Weak supplier evidence | Medium | Medium | Medium | Complete supplier due diligence |
| People-impacting use without review | High | Medium | Medium to High | Escalate before deployment |

## Evidence produced

A short readiness engagement could produce the following artefacts:

1. AI use-case register for Sundai AI workflows.
2. AI risk register with owners and next actions.
3. AI supplier due-diligence checklist.
4. AI impact assessment for one selected workflow.
5. Human oversight decision log template.
6. AI incident and complaint handling template.
7. Management readiness report with gaps and recommended priorities.

| Supplier review | Needs review | Tool due diligence should be completed |

| People-impacting AI use | High caution | Formal review required before use |

## Prioritised recommendations

### Priority 1: Create visibility

Create and maintain an AI use-case register for every Sundai AI workflow. Assign an owner to each use case.

### Priority 2: Define safe-use rules

Adopt a short internal AI-use policy covering data minimisation, restricted uses, human review, approved tools and escalation.

### Priority 3: Review the supplier/tool

Complete the supplier due-diligence checklist and document data-retention, training, access-control, audit-log and incident-notification expectations.

### Priority 4: Strengthen human oversight

Define which outputs require review before publication, sending, operational use or people-impacting decisions.

### Priority 5: Prepare incident handling

Add an AI incident and complaint handling template so the company can respond to inaccurate, harmful, unfair, confidential or unauthorised AI outputs.

## Consultant summary

The company is not necessarily using AI irresponsibly. The main gap is that AI use has grown faster than the governance layer around it.

A practical AI governance readiness process helps the organisation move from informal AI adoption to documented, owned, risk-assessed and evidence-backed AI use.
